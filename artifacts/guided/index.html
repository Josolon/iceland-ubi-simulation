An agent-based model of Iceland — 394,108 people, 27 professions, one year at a time. Every number here is a model run, always shown against the status quo in the same year.

What this is

An independent research model by Jónatan Sólon, built to explore what a basic income of this kind could do in Iceland and how much of that is known. It has no funder and argues for no outcome. Results are differences from the status-quo run in the same year, because a common drift cancels in that comparison.

What is measured

Calibrated to Hagstofa 2025: employment, wage bill, tax receipts, consumption and the profession mix. The labour-supply response is pinned to an extensive elasticity of 0.25.

Reading the marks

A * marks a number the fitted surface reproduces loosely (R² between 0.5 and 0.85); "not fitted" marks one it cannot reproduce at all, where the status-quo column still stands because it is an exact run.

What is estimated

Around thirty behavioural settings are estimates. Treat directions and thresholds as the finding, never the decimals.

Known limits

Tested against 2016–25: unemployment, output, population, migration, the policy rate, prices and the budget are reproduced through identified shocks; real wages run about 6% below the record and exports about a tenth below it. Levels drift; differences between the two runs are the reliable part. The charts come from a surface fitted to 4,300 runs of the model, not from the model itself; checked against the model's own runs at the nine scenarios, it puts the 2035 budget within about 140 ma.kr. of the model (about 40 on average), and each scenario's note gives the model's own figures. The past is not fitted: every combination on that tab is a run of the model. A run with nothing changed reproduces the status quo to the last digit.
