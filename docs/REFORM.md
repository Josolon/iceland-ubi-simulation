# The reference reform

The reform the explorer opens on, component by component, against today's rules. It is a starting point: every
component is a lever on the page and can be set back to today or anywhere in its range. The values here are the
page's scenario "300 þús., reference reform" (`yours300` in `artifacts/explorer_v3/index.html`); its results in
the model are in the [README](../README.md).

Only what the model represents is listed. The reference reform assumes the world as the last decade went (prices
rising about 4% a year, a weakening króna, rents outrunning incomes), and the status quo it is compared with runs in
that same world.

## The payment

| Component | Today | Reference reform |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly payment to every resident 18 and over | none | 300 þús. kr., untaxed, not withdrawn as earnings rise |
| Payment for each child | child benefit, income-tested | 300 þús. kr.: half to the family, half into the child's start-of-life fund, paid out at 25 |
| Indexation | benefits follow prices and about 80% of real-wage growth | prices only |
| Benefits replaced | unemployment benefit, Tryggingastofnun's old-age and disability pensions, child benefit and others (358.6 ma.kr. in 2025) | all of them |
| Top-up for disability and old-age pensioners whose income would fall | — | none (the lever *Pension top-up to today's level* at 0) |
| Eligibility | — | residents 18 and over; EEA arrivals while they work; others after five years |
| Residence registration | no means test | requires savings or an income (strictness 50%) |
| Payment withheld in prison | benefits stop after four months | from the first day |
| Payment protected from lenders; payments to foreign gambling sites blocked | protected; not blocked | protected; blocked |
| Phase-in | — | payment and taxes from the first day; public-job cuts and the pension fold over three years |

## Taxes

| Component | Today | Reference reform |
|---|---|---|
| VAT | 24% and 11%, with exemptions (about 18% in effect) | one rate of 25% |
| Input VAT reclaimable | on all business purchases | only on value-chain inputs (estimated 459.5 ma.kr. a year in full, range 385–574) |
| Income tax rates | 31.49 / 37.99 / 46.29% | unchanged |
| Personal tax credit | 72.5 þús. kr. a month | abolished |
| Property tax | about 0.59% of assessed value | plus one percentage point |
| Corporate tax | 20% | 20%; a firm in which the state holds a stake pays its return to the state as owner instead of the tax |
| Inheritance and gift tax | 10% above 6.5 m kr. | unchanged |
| Fisheries resource rent | about 24% captured | 50% |
| Customs duties | 7.7 ma.kr. | unchanged |
| Per diems (dagpeningar) | paid tax-free | abolished; documented travel costs instead |

## Pensions and leave

| Component | Today | Reference reform |
|---|---|---|
| Occupational pension funds | contributions of 15.5% of pay; pensions paid | contributions go to the state as social security; the funds' returns (3% real assumed) go to the scheme; occupational pensions stop, topped up for anyone whose pension was worth more than the payment |
| Sick pay and parental leave | the state's sick pay and the parental leave fund | employers for the period law and agreements give, then compulsory insurance |

## Behavioural settings in the reference picture

These are not policy. They are the model's estimates, applied to the status quo and the reform alike, and shown
here because the page opens with them set:

| Setting | Value | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| People change profession | 4% a year | see [PARAMETERS.md](PARAMETERS.md) |
| What work that fits is worth | 2% more produced after a move | an estimate |
| More spent when income is guaranteed | 10% | direction from Carroll (1997), Carroll and Samwick (1998); size an estimate |
| Births earlier in life | 1 year at 300 þús. | Herr (2011), Miller (2011), Bach et al. (2025); size an estimate |
| Teenagers living on their own | 5% of 16–17-year-olds | a guess |

## Not in the model

Some items associated with this kind of reform are not represented and so are not part of the reference reform as
modelled: the organisation of primary care beyond free visits, the treatment of intellectual property, changes to
schooling and universities, and any change to institutions such as the judiciary. They move no number in the model.
